Norris denies Russell pole position in Dutch GP Qualifying

2026年8月23日 · 6 分钟 · 1212 字

Lando Norris seized a spectacular pole position for the Dutch Grand Prix under the threat of rain, edging out Mercedes' George Russell and Kimi Antonelli in the final minutes of Qualifying.

The McLaren driver braved one more lap as the weather worsened at the Circuit Zandvoort, earning the top spot with a time of 1m 11.163s in a tense conclusion to the session.

While he comprehensively beat Russell by a tenth, the Mercedes pair were extremely close as they took P2 and P3, with Oscar Piastri securing P4 after he looked like the fastest driver on track for the majority of Qualifying.

Ferrari’s Lewis Hamilton and Charles Leclerc were next on the timesheets, heading Max Verstappen and Liam Lawson, who wasn’t far off the lead Red Bull, while Gabriel Bortoleto and Arvid Lindblad completed the top 10.

Alpine’s Pierre Gasly came the closest to making it into Q3, but lost out by less than a tenth and qualified ahead of his former team mate Yuki Tsunoda, who was 0.102s adrift of the lead Racing Bulls car upon his return to the grid.

Nico Hulkenberg wound up P13 for Audi, leading the second Alpine of Franco Colapinto, Haas' Esteban Ocon and Williams' Alex Albon.

In another frustrating session for Sainz, he was eliminated from Q1 with a tiny deficit of 0.022s to his team mate, settling for P17 from Aston Martin’s Fernando Alonso and Lance Stroll who are yet to extract the most from their upgraded power unit.

Ollie Bearman was another to be outqualified by his Haas counterpart as he was knocked out in P20, where he will line up just in front of Cadillac drivers Valtteri Bottas and Sergio Perez.

AS IT HAPPENED#

A few hours on from Russell’s emphatic Sprint victory, it was time for the drivers to reconvene for a crucial hour of Qualifying. The relatively few changes in the order during the earlier 24-lap event proved that track position would be key for the Grand Prix, so the pressure was on for this session.

As less than a tenth separated the top four drivers in Friday’s Sprint Qualifying, it looked like competition for the front row would be tight once again, with Russell hoping to take P1 for the third time this weekend.

The sun was trying its best to shine over Zandvoort as a queue of eager drivers formed at the end of the pit lane for the start of Q1, and it was Hulkenberg, who was not classified in the Sprint after suffering a power issue, out first for Audi to catch up on his lost track time.

With soft tyres the compound of choice, Racing Bulls' reserve Tsunoda shot to the top spot in the opening minutes, which didn’t last for long as the Ferrari and Red Bull competitors demoted him down the timesheets.

Hamilton led the field with an initial lap of 1m 13.406s - almost two seconds slower than the best efforts in Sprint Qualifying - before McLaren’s Norris shot to the top spot, followed by an even better lap from his team mate Piastri.

The first runs gradually came to a close as Russell slotted into P2 while Hulkenberg and the two Racing Bulls posted eye-catching laps to make it into the top 10 ahead of Hamilton, who locked up heading into Turn 1 and took a short trip into the gravel.

On the flip side, the drop zone heading into the final laps featured Bortoleto, the Haas duo of Bearman and Ocon, Cadillac’s Bottas and Perez, and Stroll for Aston Martin. Out of those, Bortoleto was undoubtedly the biggest surprise, leaving the Audi man with plenty to do after he made an error that ruined his lap.

Hamilton improved to P2 with just 0.085s separating the top three drivers, but it was the end of the road for Sainz, Alonso, Stroll, Bearman and the Cadillac pair, with Albon squeezing into Q2 along with Ocon while Bortoleto also lifted himself clear of the drop.

Knocked out: Sainz, Alonso, Stroll, Bearman, Bottas, Perez

Q2 – Piastri on top again but Tsunoda misses out#

There was no time to waste as the second phase of Qualifying kicked off and Russell set the early benchmark of 1m 11.959s, eclipsing his team mate’s effort by around four-tenths as Antonelli continued to find himself off the pace, even with both drivers on fresh softs.

Norris was the next to shoot into first place but was once again narrowly beaten by Piastri as both sought to capitalise on McLaren’s latest upgrades. The fast corners in the middle sector seemed to be their greatest strength, but it was only enough to edge out Ferrari’s Leclerc by a few hundredths.

Meanwhile, the usual suspects from outside the four quickest teams occupied the bottom positions shortly before the last flying laps, with Lindblad and Bortoleto hoping to cling onto a place in the top 10 to progress to Q3.

Red Bull pair Verstappen and Lawson surged into P3 and P8 respectively, with the latter impressing upon his surprise return to the squad. Those who weren’t as lucky were Gasly, Tsunoda, Hulkenberg, Colapinto, Ocon and Albon, all of whom failed to progress to the final part of Qualifying.

Knocked out: Gasly, Tsunoda, Hulkenberg, Colapinto, Ocon, Albon

Q3 – Norris snatches pole position from Mercedes#

Just 12 minutes remained, and proceedings were set to be thrown off by the risk of rain at what would normally be an ideal time for the surviving drivers to produce their best laps yet.

That meant that the opening attempts would be more important than ever, with the McLaren duo of Norris and Piastri once again leading the way before Russell split them. Leclerc then slotted into P4 ahead of Antonelli, Verstappen and Lawson, with Hamilton a disappointing P8 - how long would everyone wait to gamble on another try on slick tyres?

The rain began to fall with five minutes to go, reducing both the temperature and grip on track, but Russell still found a way to fly on his final lap. His best effort wasn’t enough to hold off a charging Norris, who improved again to seize pole position with a time of 1m 11.163s.

There was time for others to challenge the Briton, but it wasn’t to be for Ferrari as Hamilton and Leclerc could only manage fifth and sixth respectively, just behind the McLaren of Piastri.

Red Bull couldn’t close up either, with Verstappen qualifying ahead of Lawson while Bortoleto and Lindblad rounded out the order.

Key quote#

“It doesn’t get any better,” Norris said. “A little surprised honestly but, yeah, a decent lap. I was pushing because you see how close it is, so you know every one-hundredth, two-hundredths can make a big difference in the end. But just a good turnaround, a good turnaround from earlier today where I felt like we struggled a little bit. Today we certainly felt very strong. The team did a good job, so credit to them on trying to get a little bit more out of the car and it certainly helped me today.”
