The bucket-list moments from the F1 calendar in Europe

2026年9月24日 · 5 分钟 · 969 字

F1’s European summer is coming to an end. Since June, the Formula 1 paddock has travelled across the continent, taking in some of the sport’s most iconic venues, from Silverstone and Monaco to Spa-Francorchamps, alongside a new addition in the form of the Madring.

From celebrity spotting in Monaco to track invasions at Monza, every race has its own unwritten rules and bucket-list moments. The question is: have you ticked them off?

Have you ever felt like a celebrity in Monaco?#

In Monaco, everyone gets to feel like a celebrity for a weekend. Arguably the most iconic track on the Formula 1 calendar, the Monaco Grand Prix never disappoints.

The view from the balconies of the Principality’s streets while VIPs party on the many yachts in the marina is one of the most recognisable and glamorous photos of the F1 season.

Winning in Monaco has often come with a stylish celebration, including a dive in the harbour. This year, it was the turn of Kimi Antonelli, the youngest driver to win the Monaco Grand Prix - taking victory aged just 19 years, nine months and 13 days to break Lewis Hamilton’s long-standing record from 2008.

Have you ever joined the celebrations in Monza?#

The Tifosi might be dedicated supporters of Ferrari, but they also create a brilliant atmosphere for all the teams. As soon as it’s safe to do so, all the fans from the Monza grandstands love to invade the track to show their passion for the sport.

Take this year, for example. Despite Ferrari missing out on the podium, the famous red wave united behind Mercedes star Kimi Antonelli, who charged from the back of the grid to claim an emotional home victory.

The triumph made him the first Italian to win the Italian Grand Prix in 60 years, since Ludovico Scarfiotti’s success in 1966, prompting scenes of celebration rarely seen for a non-Ferrari driver at Monza.

Have you ever packed for every season in Spa?#

You can’t talk about the Belgian Grand Prix without mentioning Eau Rouge-Raidillon, one of the most iconic sequences of corners in world motorsport.

Overtaking here is a big risk but when it is done well it’s a breathtaking spectacle, which four-time World Champion Max Verstappen gave us all when he passed Antonelli for the lead on the opening lap earlier this year.

The Circuit of Spa-Francorchamps is one of the best places to visit a Grand Prix, although be sure to pack plenty of layers, a rain coat and plenty of sunscreen. There are two things that are consistent, though: excellent racing and shacks selling delicious fries with mayo.

Have you ever stayed for the Silverstone encore?#

Arguably one of the greatest tracks in the world, Silverstone is always special. As the home of the British Grand Prix, not only does it embrace its nation’s drivers, but it also knows how to put on a show.

This year saw the ‘Landostand’ where thousands of Lando Norris fans wore bright yellow to show their support to the reigning World Champion.

Then there are the concerts taking the stage after all the track action is over. Headline sets came from the likes of David Guetta, Richard Ashcroft, Chase & Status and James Arthur, adding their names to previous performers like RAYE, Fatboy Slim, Sam Fender, Black Eyed Peas and Stormzy. It’s a true festival with Robbie Williams already announced for a 2027 appearance.

Before the racing action there was that memorable Drivers' Parade, as all 22 drivers took to the famed British circuit for a different kind of four-wheeled action in LEGO minicars. Unsurprisingly, the lap featured just as much carnage as a real race, with plenty of bumps, spins and questionable racecraft along the way.

If you were at Silverstone this season, you might also have been among the lucky fans to sample some traditional English fish and chips made by British quartet Lando Norris, George Russell, Ollie Bearman and Arvid Lindblad.

With fish and chips a British favourite, the four drivers traded the track for the fryer and put their culinary skills to the test. The question is: would you have eaten what they served up?

Have you ever witnessed history in Barcelona?#

It might have been the last race for the Circuit Barcelona-Catalunya until 2028, but that didn’t stop it from being another awesome weekend for fans who got to see Lewis Hamilton winning his first race for Ferrari.

It was the same place Michael Schumacher won for the Scuderia exactly 30 years ago, and has also been the site of many first-time wins including for Verstappen in 2016 and Pastor Maldonado in 2012.

Away from the track, fans at Barcelona may have found themselves sitting alongside a mystery spectator in a Spider-Man mask during FP1, only to discover it was Drivers' Championship leader Antonelli. With Fred Vesti taking over his Mercedes for the session, the Italian opted to watch the action from a fan’s perspective in the grandstands.

Have you even admired the view in Austria?#

Situated in the Styrian mountains, the Red Bull Ring has one of the best backdrops for a race track in the world. The green and lush point of view, wherever you are at track, is an overwhelming experience as you watch the grid do laps of the short but sweet circuit.

It’s not to be underestimated just how special this place is, with so many locals opting to wear the traditional Lederhosen and Dirndl, or going bright orange in support of Verstappen.

Over the weekend - always full of action on and off the track - fans at the 2026 Austrian Grand Prix also have the opportunity to enjoy not only the Formula 1 race, but get a special sight of the scenery courtesy of an alternative type of entertainment - bungee jumping.
